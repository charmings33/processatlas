About this project ▸

AI-grounded querying of a robotic construction knowledge graph. This prototype demonstrates how large language models can be made accountable to structured research data. It operates on a knowledge graph of robotic construction and digital fabrication in Japan, comprising 140 enablers from a relational ontology (Ng et al., 2022), 50 documented cases, and a census of 217 construction robots. Natural-language questions are answered through a hybrid AI pipeline: neural embedding retrieval identifies the relevant entities, graph traversal supplies their exact relations, and a large language model composes the answer under a strict grounding constraint, in which every claim carries a citation that links back to a specific edge in the graph, together with its literature source and evidence level. The interface makes this epistemic structure visible. Statistically derived co-occurrences and literature-cited dependencies are rendered as distinct layers, so users can see which parts of an answer rest on asserted mechanisms and which on correlation. AI also participates in growing the graph itself, proposing new dependency edges that remain visibly flagged as unvalidated until reviewed. This deliberate human-in-the-loop design lets AI accelerate knowledge work while verification and provenance remain in human hands.
